Why is the Ethiopian year different?

The Ethiopian calendar is based on the Alexandrian or Coptic calendar. It is typically 7 to 8 years behind the Gregorian calendar that is used in most of the world. This is because the Ethiopian calendar has an extra 13th month (Pagume) and calculates the birth of Jesus Christ differently.

When the Gregorian calendar is in September, the Ethiopian calendar celebrates its New Year (Enkutatash) on September 11th (or 12th in leap years).
